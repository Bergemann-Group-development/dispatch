## 4. Two-stage NLTE synthesis: departures first, then the SED from the full line list

**What the code already had.**

- **Stage 1.** A departures run (the `departures` preset, or any NLTE run) saves
  `save/pop_<atom>_*` and the atmosphere. Several atoms can be solved in one run
  (`atom_file` takes a list).
- **Stage 2.** A spectrum run on those departures: the `read_departures` preset
  (`atmos_format='save'`, `atmos_file=<stage-1 run>`, `read_departures=T`,
  `&rt_iteration_params maxiter=0`). This is what `tumult.synthesis` does for NLTE
  requests (`api._spec_jobs`, `snapshot.spectrum_settings`).
- **Default behaviour.** By default (`exclude_from_line_list=T`) an atom's species is
  dropped from the line list, and the spectrum takes that element's lines from the
  model atom. With `exclude_from_line_list=F` only exact duplicates (same frequency and
  log gf) are dropped. With `disable_bb=T` the atom keeps its levels and continua but not
  its lines.
- **Level matching.** It is not done through the line list's `i j` columns: VALD records
  carry none. HOP gives each line of the element the departures of the departure-set
  levels nearest in energy (`HOP/linelist_mod.f90`, `find_closest`; energies tagged
  100 x stage, lower level at E_low, upper at E_low + h nu). There is no tolerance and no
  report of a poor match.

**What did not work, fixed in f63e2fc95.** Two atoms' precomputed departures could not
be read in one run, and `disable_bb` did nothing for classic MULTI atoms (mg86e,
fe607a). Each species' lines were therefore counted twice: once from the atom, once
from the line list.

**Campaign route.**

- **Stage 1** is the batch-2 departure runs, already done: Mg and Fe separately, each
  with the phase-1 rule (section 5 explains why not jointly).
- **Stage 2** (`make_nml.py batch2_sed`, `pilot_sed`) reads the combined departures:
  `runs/b2/dep/<model>` links the Fe run's atmosphere and departures and the Mg run's
  departures.
  - It synthesises the HRS windows at R = 2e5 from the full FGK line list, with
    `&atom_params atom_file=mg86e,fe607a disable_bb=T exclude_from_line_list=F`.
  - Every Mg and Fe line of VALD, Fe II included, takes NLTE level populations.
- **LTE partner.** The same run's LTE spectrum (`crop(LTE=True)`) has the same line list,
  mesh and wavelengths. On the Sun it reproduces the plain batch-1a LTE run to 0.2 mA
  (ifort, job 2178303).
- **Cost.** A stage-2 SED takes 20 s on one thread and 0.7 GiB.

**Level-match fractions** in the HRS windows (`jobs/level_match.py`, job 2178307,
`vald_atoms_abo.txt`). "Both" means that the lower and the upper level each lie within the
given energy of an atom level.

| Species | Lines | Both within 0.01 eV | 0.05 eV | 0.2 eV | Atom levels of the stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mg I | 37 | 54 % | 92 % | 97 % | 85 |
| Mg II | 33 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 (ground only) |
| Fe I | 7366 | 84 % | 91 % | 99 % | 548 |
| Fe II | 8570 | 1 % | 19 % | 100 % | 58 (coarse terms) |

**Spot checks.**

- **Correct matches.** Each strong line maps onto the right terms within 0.001 eV:
  - the Mg b triplet onto 3p3P0,1,2 - 4s3S;
  - Mg I 5528 and 5711 onto 3p1P - 4d1D and 5s1S;
  - Fe I 5232.94 onto z7P4 - e7D5, 5250.21 onto a5D0 - z7D1, 6252.56 onto a3H6 - z3G5;
  - Fe I 4045.81 onto a3F4 - y3F4.
- **Fe II.** The atom's Fe II levels are coarse terms. Fe II 5169.03 lands on levels
  0.08-0.10 eV away, and most Fe II lines on levels 0.01-0.2 eV away. Since b(Fe II) is
  close to 1 throughout, the effect on the flux is small. It is still approximate and is
  not checked per line.
- **Mg II.** mg86e has only the Mg II ground level, so every Mg II line takes
  b_i = b_j = b(Mg II ground). The HRS windows hold only weak, high-excitation Mg II lines.
- **Unmatched lines.** The ~16 % of Fe I lines (and 46 % of Mg I) beyond 0.01 eV
  are high-excitation lines whose levels the atom merges or lacks. They take their
  neighbours' departures silently. A warning with a tolerance in `find_closest` would make
  this visible; that is a candidate change, not made here.

**The pilot.** The pilot's NLTE spectra (1D and 3D) came from the departure runs
themselves, i.e. from the model atoms' lines without Fe II and with the atoms' own gf
values. For consistency with batch 2 they are redone through stage 2, reusing the saved
departures with no new solve: the 14 1D runs in job 2178304 and the finished 3D Mg
snapshots in job 2178305. The 3D Fe snapshots follow once they exist, and the further
3D Mg snapshots as they finish (rerun `make_nml.py pilot_sed`, submit
`jobs/p1sed_3d.sh`, about 20-30 min per 3D SED on 24 threads).

## 5. Departures of several atoms in one run versus separately

Local gfortran timing, one thread, nz = 64, departures only. Each case runs Mg (mg86e),
Fe (fe607a) and O (o41f) separately, at their own rules (Mg and O: convlim 1e-4 and
convmax 1e-5; Fe: 1e-3 and 1e-4), and all three in one run under both rules:

| Model | Mg + Fe + O separately | Joint, strict rule | Joint, Fe rule | Peak RSS separate / joint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sun (p5777 g4.4) | 63.2 s | 101.8 s | 70.6 s | 1.21 / 1.28 GB |
| s5000 g2.0 [Fe/H] = -3 | 85.3 s | 126.3 s | 89.6 s | 1.21 / 1.29 GB |
| p4000 g4.5 | 45.3 s | 69.0 s | 53.1 s | 1.20 / 1.28 GB |
| <3D> t50g25m20 | 62.7 s | 95.5 s | 68.0 s | 1.20 / 1.28 GB |

- **Cost.** The joint run is slower: 1.5x the sum of the separate runs at the strict
  rule, 1.1x at the Fe rule. Fe dominates either way. Joint runs would also impose one
  rule on all atoms.
- **The departures differ beyond convergence.** max |Delta log b| between joint and
  separate is 0.04-0.09 dex for Mg and Fe and up to 0.18 dex for O (Sun). The 95th
  percentile is 1-2 % for Mg and 0.2-0.9 % for Fe.
  - The strict and the loose joint runs agree with each other, so this is not a
    convergence effect. It is the coupling: in a joint run each atom's transitions are
    part of the others' radiation field (overlapping bound-bound and bound-free).
  - Which is "right" depends on whether the separate runs' background treats those
    elements equivalently. The joint run is the physically more complete one.
- **Decision for batch 2.** The departure runs stay separate: they are already done, the
  route is faster, and it matches the pilot. Whether the campaign should switch to joint
  solves for consistency is a question for the user (section 10).
- **More elements.** Of the extra atoms, O (o41f) converges in 0.4-0.9 s per model on
  one thread. The others in `storm/data/new_atoms` (Al, C, Na, ...) were not tried in
  this session. Adding cheap atoms to batch 2 would cost about 1 core-h per atom for the
  1418 models.

## 6. <3D> departures mapped onto 3D snapshots

**Existing support.** None for mapping. `read_departures` takes the departures and the
atmosphere from the same saved run. `tumult.departures.DepartureProfile` goes the other
way, averaging a cube's departures onto its mean log tau_500 scale.

**What was needed, and is now `jobs/map_departures.py`** (about 100 lines, no Fortran
change):

- **Mapping.** For each cell of a snapshot it interpolates log b(log tau_500) of the 1D
  <3D> run per level, linearly in log tau_500 and held constant beyond the 1D range.
  log tau_500 is each column's vertical optical depth, from the saved 500 nm opacity, as
  `tumult` computes it for both runs.
- **Output.** It writes `save/pop_<el>_*` in the layout of a 3D NLTE run, unrotated copy
  only, beside symlinks of the snapshot's saved atmosphere. A `read_departures` run
  projects the ray-aligned copies itself (`nlte_patch_t%project_populations`, called for
  precomputed departures as well).
- **Validation** (job 2178301). Writing the true 3D departures of t5777g44m00 s00459
  through the tool reproduces the original files bit for bit in all 8 patches.
- **First look**, the same snapshot with the Sun's <3D> Mg departures mapped onto it.
  Horizontal means, log b of the four lowest Mg I levels:

  | log tau_500 | true 3D NLTE | mapped <3D> |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 | -0.001 | -0.000 |
  | -2 | -0.002 | -0.002 |
  | -4 | +0.016 to +0.020 | +0.083 to +0.087 |

  The mapping keeps the deep layers and misses the upper photosphere, where the 3D
  radiation field and the horizontal inhomogeneity matter.
- **Comparison set-up** (`make_nml.py pilot_sed`, job 2178305). Per finished 3D Mg
  snapshot, two 3D line-list SEDs with the `3d` preset:
  - (a) on the snapshot's own 3D departures (true 3D NLTE);
  - (b) on the mapped <3D> departures.

  Both carry their 3D LTE partner (`LTE=True`). The 1D MARCS and <3D> stage-2 SEDs of
  the same model come from job 2178304.

  `jobs/pilot_compare.py OUT.csv` turns the finished runs into per-line abundance
  differences against 3D NLTE (dA_map, dA_3d_lte, dA_{marcs,av3d}_{nlte,lte}). It reads
  the true 3D, mapped, 1D MARCS and 1D <3D> spectra of each Mg line, with the
  curve-of-growth slope of section 7.

  **No 3D comparison numbers yet.** The four maps exist; the first true-3D SED started
  at 07:15. Run `pilot_compare.py` once 2178311 has finished.

  The output pass dominates every NLTE 3D spectrum: about 3 h against 15 min for the
  same snapshot in LTE. Its scattering iterations (`&rt_output_params maxiter=3`) and

## 7. First-look results: Delta_NLTE and Delta_<3D>

**Method.**

- **Corrections.** Abundance-like corrections from equivalent widths
  (`jobs/delta_maps.py`, tables `$C/runs/b2/delta_{A,B,A2,B2}.csv`).
  - EW: the integral of 1 - F/F_cont over a fixed window around each line. Windows are
    0.3-0.5 Å for weak lines; 1-1.5 Å for Mg b and the strong Fe I lines, which therefore
    give core EWs.
  - Conversion to dex: dA = -Delta log EW / s. The slope s = dlog EW/dA comes from the
    batch-1a [X/Fe] = +-0.3 sets of the same MARCS model (for B, the nearest batch-1a
    model).
- **Lines.**
  - Mg I 5172.7, 5528.4, 5711.1.
  - Fe I: 5232.9 and 5269.5 (strong), 5250.2 (low EP), 5383.4 (high EP), 6136.6,
    6252.6, 6430.8, 6593.9.
  - Fe II (stage 2 only): 5234.6, 6247.6, 6432.7, 6456.4, 6516.1.
- **Which spectra.** The figures and numbers below use the stage-2 SEDs (full line
  list on the departures, A2/B2).

The A/B tables from the departure runs' own model-atom spectra agree with them:

| Lines | 95th percentile of A2 - A (dex) |
|---|---|
| Fe I | up to 0.016 |
| Mg I 5528 | 0.03 |
| Mg b | 0.06 |

So the route matters at the 0.01-0.06 dex level, mostly through the different gf values
and blends.

## 8. Star placement (crude 1D LTE labels, not science results)

![placement](figures/phase2_placement.png)

**Method** (`jobs/place_stars.py`, `$C/runs/place/labels.csv`).

- **Data.** 3096 HRS spectra (0201, 0301, 0401, 0408; one 0408 file is empty).
- **Grid.** The 1177 batch-1a LTE references, broadened to R = 20,000 in each arm.
- **Fit.** chi2 over the grid nodes, with a straight-line continuum factor per arm, an
  error floor of 0.02, and Ca II H&K and H-alpha cores masked.
- **Two passes.**
  - Free: Teff, log g and [Fe/H] all free. Giants come out at dwarf gravities: log g is
    0.66 dex too high for stars with photometric log g < 3.5, against 0.22 dex for
    dwarfs.
  - Final: log g restricted to within 0.5 dex of the Gaia value (M_G, BC_G from Andrae
    et al. 2018, 1 Msun; parallax S/N > 5).
- **Gaia Teff.** Mucciarelli, Bellazzini & Massari (2021) colour relation, E(BP-RP) =
  0.432 A0. Spectroscopic Teff is 168 K hotter than this (sd 214 K).
- **Per star.** One star is one point, its highest-S/N spectrum: 2025 stars, 1566 of
  them with log g held at Gaia. Labels are grid nodes; the weighted means barely move off
  the best node.

**Results.**

- **Coverage.** 98 % of the stars have a 3D Stagger model within one grid step
  (250 K, 0.5 dex in log g, 0.5 dex in [Fe/H]).
- **0408 and 0401** (1515 stars) are turn-off dwarfs and subgiants: median 6250 / 4.0 /
  -0.25 and 5750 / 4.0 / -0.25. They sit in the densest part of the 3D grid. 5 % of all
  stars land at the 6750 K grid edge, so the turn-off may extend hotter than batch 1a.
- **0301** (8 stars, S/N 165) are clump/RGB giants near 4750 / 2.5 / -0.5.
- **0201** (502 stars, median S/N 25) is the problem set. Its labels are unreliable:
  - 80 % pin at the grid edge [Fe/H] = -4, mostly at log g 5;

## 10. Decisions for the user

1. **SED benchmark.** Add the `3d` sampling keys to `benchmarks/paper/sed_4000-9000.nml`
   (section 1) and rerun it for the paper table. The 19 min / 37 GB entry was not
   measured with the current namelist.
2. **3D Fe time limit.** Ask the admins to extend 2178278_0-2 beyond 48 h, or accept the
   risk; decide by Sunday evening from the iteration tables. More generally, at about
   1 h per iteration the 14 Fe snapshots take until mid-week. Reduce to the first
   snapshot per model (tasks 0-6) if node time is needed elsewhere.
3. **Joint or separate departures.** Joint Mg+Fe+O solves are 1.1-1.5x slower than
   separate runs but change b by up to 0.05-0.09 dex (Mg, Fe) through the coupled
   radiation field (section 5). Which should the campaign use? Batch 2 is separate.
4. **Level matching.** Fe II lines map onto coarse Fe II terms, and about 16 % of Fe I
   lines lie more than 0.01 eV from any atom level. All of these take neighbouring
   departures silently. Should `find_closest` get a tolerance with a warning, and should
   the Fe II corrections of section 7 be trusted before an Fe atom with finer Fe II
   levels is used?
5. **Delta_<3D> for cool giants** (+0.3 to +0.5 dex for Fe I). Should the twins be
   rerun with the <3D> files' microturbulence (or both at one fixed vmic) before these
   numbers are used?
6. **4MOST 0201 labels.** Obtain the photometry or pipeline labels for the 429 newer
   0201 spectra, and decide whether to extend batch 1a below [Fe/H] = -4 and above
   6750 K.
7. **Node83** has a stale `/opt/intel` mount (admins).
8. **The pilot's NLTE spectra.** Redo them via stage 2 (queued for the existing
   departures, section 4) and use those for the Delta terms. The one-run model-atom
