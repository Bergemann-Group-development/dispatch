## 3. Phase-2 anomalies: Fe II corrections and NLTE emission

### 3a. Fe II Delta_NLTE of -0.03 to -0.13 dex at low metallicity

The cause is the Fe II part of fe607a, not the level matching.

- **The atom.** fe607a has 548 Fe I levels, 58 Fe II levels and the Fe III ground.
  - All 12635 lines are Fe I lines. The Fe II levels are coupled only by collisions and by
    photoionisation to Fe III (58 continua).
  - The Fe II levels are evenly spaced super-levels (0, 0.25, 0.98, 1.64, ... 16.2 eV).
- **Matching distances** (`anomalies.py feii`, job 2178322):

  | Line | Lower and upper level | dE |
  |---|---|---|
  | 5234.6 | 558, 567 | 0.05 / 0.09 eV |
  | 6247.6 | 561, 568 | 0.02 / 0.04 eV |
  | 6456.4 | 561, 568 | 0.03 / 0.02 eV |
  | 6516.1 | 557, 565 | 0.10 / 0.06 eV |

  6432.7 is left out: my lookup took a different Fe II line at the same wavelength.
- **Departures over 1418 models.** All excited Fe II super-levels share one departure
  coefficient at every depth: log b_lower = log b_upper to 1e-3. So S_line = B
  exactly, and the matching distance has no effect.
  - What changes is the line opacity. In cool metal-poor giants the excited Fe II levels
    sit at log b = +0.15 to +0.20 (40-60 % overpopulated) between log tau -2 and -1,
    while the Fe II ground stays at +0.01.
- **The corrections follow that b.**
  - Over all models and the four lines, Delta_NLTE = -0.91 log b_lower(log tau = -1)
    + 0.009 (correlation -0.89).
  - The extremes (-0.12 to -0.13 dex: s4250/s4500, log g 1.5-2, [Fe/H] = -4) have
    log b_lower = +0.15 to +0.16.
- **Artefact or physics.** An excited Fe II population that runs 40-60 % ahead of the
  ground is an artefact of an Fe II model without radiative bound-bound coupling.
  - In this atom only weak collisions (few electrons at [Fe/H] = -4) tie those levels to
    the ground, and recombination from Fe III feeds them.
  - In complete Fe II atoms the published 1D NLTE Fe II corrections are within a few
    0.01 dex at these metallicities.
  - At solar metallicity the same mechanism gives log b = -0.007 and +0.01 to +0.02 dex,
    which is harmless.
- **Recommendation.** Treat the fe607a Fe II corrections at [Fe/H] <= -2 as unreliable:
  use LTE for Fe II there, or adopt an Fe atom with Fe II transitions (decision 4).

### 3b. NLTE flux above the continuum

Two runs, two different causes.

- **`b2/n1d/s4000_g+1.0_m1.0_t02_z-4.00_fe`** (MARCS, max 1.061). The emission is
  physical within the model atom; numerically it is fine.
  - It sits in about 20 high-excitation Fe I lines at 3925-4067 Å whose upper levels lie
    at 5.6-6.4 eV, e.g. 3945.1 (levels 33-224) and 3984.0 (32-211).
  - Their upper levels are overpopulated: log b_up = +0.6 to +0.9 at log tau -2..-4.
    Their lower levels are overionised (log b_lo = -0.4 to -0.9). So S/B = b_up/b_lo
    reaches 10-50 in the line-forming layers. In a 2400-2900 K outer atmosphere that
    exceeds the continuum source function at 4000 Å.
  - The run converged, and b(z) is smooth.
  - Whether real metal-poor giants show this depends on the UV pumping of the high Fe I
    levels, which this atom and a 1D, line-unblanketed UV field may overstate. These lines
    should be masked in training rather than trusted.
- **`b2/av3d/t55g40m40/fe`** (<3D>, max 1.056). The same lines in emission (3984.0,
  4007.3), but here the model is the problem: the depth scale of the metal-poor <3D>
  runs is compressed.
  - In this run the top of the atmosphere sits at log tau_500 = -1.7, against -5 in the
    Stagger average. Its 3544 K layer sits at -1.66 instead of -2.7.
  - **The tau-scale check** (`anomalies.py tauscale`, all 206 averages) compares, per
    run, the log tau_500 that dispatch.x computes against the file's at the same
    temperature.

    | [Fe/H] | Median run log tau at file -3 | Median run top |
    |---|---|---|
    | >= -2 | -2.9 | -4.4 to -5.6 |
    | -4 | -2.35 | -3.4 |

    The worst cases are t50g35m40, t50g30m40, t45g25m40, t55g40m40 and t65g45m40: top
    -1.8 to -2.0, and file -3 maps to -1.5 to -1.7. Most [Fe/H] = -3 averages and a few at
    -2 are affected above log tau -2. At log tau -1 and 0 the scales agree to within 0.2 everywhere.
  - **Likely cause.** The MULTI-format averages carry T and n_e but no density, and
    dispatch.x derives the density from (T, n_e) with its EOS. In a metal-poor
    atmosphere n_e depends steeply on T. A horizontally averaged n_e is then far above the
    n_e of the averaged T, and the EOS returns too high a density, hence too much
    opacity in the upper layers.
  - **Consequence.** Lines and NLTE effects that form above log tau ~ -1.5 in the
    [Fe/H] <= -3 <3D> runs (the strong lines, Mg b, the Fe I emission here) come from a
    distorted model. The phase-2 Delta_<3D> and NLTE x <3D> numbers at [Fe/H] <= -3
    carry this. Decision 5 asks how to fix it.

## 8. Compressibility of the LTE opacity spectra (batch 1b)

**Setup** (`opacity_pca.py`, vis4, 10 min).

- y = log10(chi/chi_cont) on the 64347-point HRS grid: the line opacity relative to the
  continuum.
- 512 training and 256 held-out points per composition, all 8 compositions
  ([Fe/H] = +0.5 to -4) in one basis.
- The held-out "photosphere" subset is 4000-7000 K and rho = 1e-9..1e-6 g/cm3
  (276 points).

| Components | Explained variance | Median abs dy | 99th pct abs dy | Max abs dy (photosphere) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.953 | 0.020 | 0.32 | 1.6 |
| 32 | 0.9965 | 0.0065 | 0.090 | 0.85 |
| 64 | 0.9993 | 0.0027 | 0.040 | 0.36 |
| 128 | 0.99989 | 0.0011 | 0.017 | 0.17 |
| 256 | 0.999987 | 0.00036 | 0.0058 | 0.072 |

- **Shape of the spectrum.** It is compressible, but slowly: there is no sharp knee.
  64 components give a 99th-percentile error of 0.04 dex (10 % in line opacity),
  128 components 0.017 dex, 256 components 0.006 dex.
- **The tail.** The maxima are 5-10x the 99th percentile and sit at line cores of points
  near the edges of the sampled (T, rho) region.
- **For the microphysics-transfer experiment.** A linear basis of a few hundred
  components across (T, rho, [Fe/H]) reaches the percent level. Per-composition bases, or
  a nonlinear decoder, are the obvious next tests.
- **Limits.** 4096 training spectra limit the rank that can be resolved, and the held-out
  set is the same sampling as the training set.

3. **Stage-2 3D SED cost.** At 3.7 h on 24 threads, the 54 pilot SEDs need about 200 h of
   node88. That needs one of:
   - profiling and trimming the output pass (`&rt_output_params maxiter=3` scattering
     iterations; phase 2 measured 15 min for the same snapshot in LTE);
   - moving the SEDs to the pilot nodes as they free up;
   - dropping the mapped-<3D> SEDs for the second snapshots.
4. **Fe II in NLTE.** fe607a's excited Fe II super-levels are overpopulated by 40-60 % in
   metal-poor giants, with no radiative coupling. Options: use LTE for Fe II at
   [Fe/H] <= -2, or adopt an Fe atom with Fe II transitions before Fe II corrections
   enter training.
5. **Metal-poor <3D> averages.** The MULTI-format files have no density, and the
   n_e-derived density compresses their tau scale above log tau -2 at [Fe/H] <= -3. A fix
   needs the averages with density (the Stagger <3D> files exist in other formats; a
   `multi1d` reader option to take rho), or recomputing n_e from (T, P) with the
   campaign's EOS. Until then, Delta_<3D> and the mapped-<3D> departures at [Fe/H] <= -3
   are suspect above log tau -1.5.
6. **Delta_3D convention.** Delta_3D for strong lines depends on the 1D vmic. Should the
   campaign compare against 1D at a vmic fitted to the 3D weak lines, restrict Delta_3D to
   weak lines, or keep the MARCS 1/2 km/s convention and store vmic as a training label?
